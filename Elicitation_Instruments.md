# MAUMart Elicitation Instruments

These are the actual instruments to take into the field -- Section 3 of
the coursework report gave a *reasoned baseline* (what fact-finding would
likely find, based on how MAU students already trade informally); this
document is what turns that into real primary research. Run these, then
replace the "Expected/likely finding" lines in Section 3 with what
respondents actually said.

## Before you start: a short ethics note

Even informal coursework fieldwork should follow basic good practice:

- **Voluntary participation.** No one should feel obligated to take part
  because you're a coursemate, colleague, or in a position of authority
  over them.
- **Anonymity in reporting.** Don't attribute specific quotes to named
  individuals in the report unless they've explicitly agreed to be
  identified. "Several respondents said..." is safer than naming names.
- **Say what it's for.** Read (or paraphrase) the consent script below
  before every interview and at the top of the questionnaire.
- **No sensitive data collection.** These instruments deliberately don't
  ask for anything beyond what's needed for the requirements-gathering
  purpose -- don't add questions about health, finances, or other
  personal circumstances beyond shopping/selling behaviour.

### Consent script (read or paraphrase before each interview)

> "I'm gathering requirements for a university coursework project on a
> campus marketplace app for MAU students and vendors. This will take
> about [10-15] minutes. There are no right or wrong answers -- I'm
> interested in your actual experience buying/selling on campus. Your
> name won't be attached to anything in the report unless you say
> that's fine. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Is it
> okay if I take notes as we talk?"

## Instrument 1: Buyer (Student) Interview Guide

**Target:** 15-20 students across a mix of departments/levels and hostel
vs. off-campus residents, since their connectivity and delivery needs differ.
**Length:** ~10-15 minutes, semi-structured -- follow up on interesting answers rather than reading verbatim.

1. Walk me through the last time you bought something from another
   student or a campus vendor (not a big platform like Jumia). How did
   you find it, and how did you pay?
2. Where do you currently look when you want to buy something on
   campus -- WhatsApp groups, Facebook, word of mouth, physically
   walking around?
3. Have you ever been unhappy with something you bought this way (wrong
   item, no delivery, seller went silent)? What did you do about it?
4. What would make you trust a seller you've never met before?
5. Would you ever pay online in advance for something on campus, or do
   you prefer to pay when you receive it? Why?
6. What device do you mostly use to browse/shop -- phone, laptop, both?
   How reliable is your data/internet, honestly?
7. If a single app existed where MAU students and campus vendors listed
   everything (books, food, electronics, services, etc.), what would
   make you actually use it instead of your current WhatsApp groups?
8. What would stop you from using it?
9. Are there things you currently *can't* easily find or buy on campus
   that you wish you could?
10. (If they've sold anything themselves) What was that experience
    like, and would you want a proper storefront instead of posting in
    a group?

## Instrument 2: Vendor / Informal Seller Interview Guide

**Target:** 8-10 people currently selling something on or near campus --
food vendors, phone/electronics sellers, tailors, print/photocopy
services, etc. Include at least one from each major category you plan
to launch with.
**Length:** ~15-20 minutes.

1. What do you currently sell, and who are your main customers?
2. How do people currently find out about what you sell -- word of
   mouth, a WhatsApp/Facebook page, a physical location, all of the above?
3. How do you currently handle payment? Cash, transfer, both? Any
   problems with that?
4. How do you currently handle delivery, if any?
5. Have you ever had a dispute with a customer (wrong order, no-show,
   non-payment)? How was it resolved?
6. If there were a single app where MAU students could find and order
   from you, what would you want it to handle for you (payments?
   delivery coordination? just visibility?)?
7. Would you be willing to pay a small commission per sale in exchange
   for that visibility and payment handling? What would feel fair?
8. What would make you *not* want to use a platform like this (too much
   admin, fees too high, don't trust it to pay out, etc.)?
9. How do you currently prove to a new customer that you're legitimate
   (reviews, referrals, just reputation)?
10. (If applicable) Do you sell anything that might need special
    permission or licensing (food safety, medication, etc.)? How is
    that currently handled?

## Instrument 3: Student Questionnaire (structured, for broader reach)

Distribute via a form tool (Google Forms or similar) to reach more
respondents than interviews alone -- aim for 50+ responses if possible
for the numbers to mean anything. Keep it under 5 minutes to complete.

1. What's your primary device for browsing the internet? *(Phone /
   Laptop / Both equally)*
2. How would you describe your typical data/internet access on campus?
   *(Reliable and affordable / Reliable but expensive / Unreliable /
   Mostly Wi-Fi only)*
3. Which of these have you bought from another student or informal
   campus vendor in the last 3 months? *(select all that apply)*
   Books/study materials · Electronics · Fashion · Food/snacks ·
   Groceries/provisions · Services (printing, tailoring, etc.) ·
   None of these
4. How do you currently pay for things bought this way? *(Cash on
   delivery / Bank transfer in advance / Mix depending on the seller /
   I don't buy this way)*
5. Rank these in order of what matters most when buying from someone
   you don't know *(drag to rank, or number 1-5)*: Price · Delivery
   speed · Seller's reviews/reputation · Ability to pay on delivery ·
   Return/refund policy
6. If a MAUMart-style app existed, how likely would you be to use it?
   *(Very likely / Somewhat likely / Unsure / Unlikely)*
7. What would most increase your trust in a vendor on such an app?
   *(open text)*
8. Is there anything you currently can't easily buy on campus that you
   wish you could? *(open text)*
9. Would you be interested in selling something yourself through such
   an app? *(Yes / Maybe / No)*
10. Any other comments? *(open text)*

## Instrument 4: Observation Checklist (existing WhatsApp/Facebook groups)

Use this while reviewing 2-3 active MAU-affiliated buy-and-sell groups.
Don't join groups you're not already a member of or a plausible
participant in -- observe what's already visible to you.

- [ ] How many posts per day, roughly, and across how many categories?
- [ ] What information do sellers typically include (price? photos?
      condition? delivery terms?) -- and what do they typically leave out?
- [ ] How do buyers ask questions -- in the group, or via DM?
- [ ] Is there any visible dispute or complaint in the last week's
      history? How was it handled -- by the seller, a group admin, or
      not at all?
- [ ] How long does it typically take a seller to respond to an
      interested buyer?
- [ ] Is there any form of seller reputation visible (repeat sellers
      people vouch for, admin-verified tags, etc.)?
- [ ] Roughly what share of posts are: goods for sale / services / "looking for X" requests?

## Instrument 5: Institutional Stakeholder Interview (MAU ICT / Student Affairs)

Only needed if you're pursuing this beyond a coursework prototype toward
something that would actually operate under the university's name.
**Length:** ~20 minutes.

1. Is there an existing digital services policy that something like
   this would need to comply with?
2. Who would need to sign off on a platform operating under MAU's name
   for commerce between students?
3. Are there existing authentication systems (student portal, matric
   number login) this could integrate with, rather than building a
   separate login?
4. What's the university's stance on data protection compliance for
   student-facing digital services -- is there a designated data
   protection officer or equivalent to consult?
5. Would there be any objection to vendor categories like food or
   informal retail operating through a university-affiliated platform?
6. What existing support channels (ICT helpdesk, etc.) would need to be
   aware of this if students started relying on it?

## Feeding this back into the report

Once you've run these:

1. Replace each "Expected/likely finding" line in Section 3.1-3.5 with
   what respondents actually said (aggregate, don't quote by name unless
   consented).
2. Revisit Section 3.6's synthesis -- confirm whether the four
   SRS-driving conclusions still hold, or whether real data shifted them
   (e.g., if trust turns out to matter less than expected, or a category
   nobody asked about turns out to be in high demand).
3. If findings meaningfully change scope (a category to add/drop, a
   payment method to prioritize), note that as a documented SRS
   revision in Section 4.3 (Constraints & Assumptions) rather than
   silently editing the original requirements.
